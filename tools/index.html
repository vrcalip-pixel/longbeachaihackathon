The AI tools guide

Gemini and Canva are the official toolkit. They are free, they run in a browser, and anyone 13 or older can use them. Beyond that, your squad may use any tool you already have an account for — as long as it costs you nothing today and you meet its age rule. This page sorts the options by what kind of model they are, so you can pick the right tool for the job instead of forcing one tool to do everything.

Three rules for any tool

Before you open anything

  • Age rules are real. 13+ tools are open to everyone here. 18+ tools are for LBCC college students only — high schoolers, don't sign up for them even if a mentor has an account.
  • Never type real private information about a real person — no names with health, money, or home details attached. Make your person up, or use yourself with the details changed.
  • Free means free today. If a tool asks for a card, close it. Trials that need payment later count as paid.

No account that works? Every coach holds a personal Google account for exactly this. Ask.

Four kinds of model

Know what you're asking for

Every "AI tool" is a wrapper around one of these. Match the model to the job and your build gets faster; mismatch it and you spend an hour fighting a tool that was never going to do what you wanted.

1 · Large language models

Text in, text out — and reasoning

Chat assistants. They read, write, summarize, plan, role-play a user, and can be set up with custom instructions to act as a specific assistant.

Use for: understanding the brief, interviewing your imaginary user, drafting messages, building a custom assistant (a Gemini Gem), writing your pitch.

Watch out: confidently wrong. Check anything factual.

2 · Image models

Words in, pictures out

Generate or edit images from a description: app screens, product shots, posters, diagrams, characters.

Use for: a mockup of the thing you're proposing, a visual for the pitch, a map or a poster.

Watch out: text inside images is often garbled; real people's faces are off-limits.

3 · Video & audio models

Short clips, voices, music

Turn a prompt or an image into a few seconds of video, or generate narration and music.

Use for: a 10-second "day in the life" of your person to open the pitch, a voiceover for a demo.

Watch out: slow and credit-limited. Never let it eat build time. Most free tiers give you one or two tries.

4 · Coding & app builders

Describe an app, get a working one

Models that write code. The friendly versions ("vibe coding" tools) hide the code and give you a clickable prototype from a plain-English description.

Use for: a working prototype the judges can tap — the strongest thing you can show.

Watch out: most of the good ones are 18+. Canva Code is the one open to everyone.

The official toolkit

Why Gemini and Canva

We picked them because they are the only pair that is free, needs no card, runs on a school lab PC or a phone, covers three of the four model types, and is open to every student in the room. That last point ruled out several stronger tools.

Gemini — think, draft, build an assistant

gemini.google.com · sign in with a personal Google account (school accounts often have it switched off).

  1. Paste your challenge brief and ask it to interview you as your person.
  2. Ask for three ways AI could help — then pick one and argue with it.
  3. Open Gems (left sidebar) → New Gem → give it a name, a role, and instructions. That is a build: a custom assistant your person could actually use.
  4. It also makes images from a prompt. Good enough for a mockup.

Canva — prototype, mock up, pitch

canva.com · free account, no card, 13+.

  1. Search templates for "app mockup" or "website" and lay out your screens.
  2. Try Canva Code (Create → Code, or the AI tab): describe the app in plain words and get a clickable prototype you can put on screen.
  3. Build the pitch here too: three slides, one page, or a flyer. Everyone in the squad speaks.
  4. Its AI tools also do images and short video clips inside the same account.

A simple plan

One tool per job

UnderstandGemini — paste the brief, ask it to be your person, ask what a Tuesday looks like for them.
DecideGemini — three ways AI could help. Pick one. Write your problem sentence.
BuildA Gem (assistant) or a Canva Code prototype or Canva mockup screens. One is enough.
ShowCanva slides: the person, the problem, the build, the risks, what's next. Three minutes.
