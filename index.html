What it is

Six industries. One day. Whatever you can build.

Long Beach City College and iUrban Teen are bringing students from across the region together for a single day of applied problem-solving. Teams pick a challenge, use generative and no-code AI tools to build a working prototype or a defensible plan, and pitch it before the day is out.

You don't need to code. You don't need a team. You need to care about a problem enough to spend a day on it.

The challenge board

Pick your fight.

Every track opens with a real brief written with industry partners. Tap any card to see where AI actually goes to work. Final briefs are released on event day — these are the shape of them.

How the day works

Big tracks. Small squads. Everyone's hands on the work.

Every student joins one of six tracks of about twenty. Inside a track, you work in a build squad of six — small enough that nobody spectates. Your track coach runs the room. Roaming AI mentors handle the tools.

Judging

Scored on the thinking, not the polish.

Three judges score six finalists against a published rubric. Judges don't coach, mentor, or advise during the event, and recuse themselves from any squad they're connected to.

Heads up on the attendance policy: the event is free, but iUrban Teen's policy charges a $25 no-show fee if you register and don't show up without telling anyone. Plans change and that's completely fine — just let them know at least 24 hours ahead and there's no fee.

Who's behind it

Two coasts of the same coastline.

A Seattle-founded youth STEM program and a Southern California college, running the same play: get students in front of real work, early.

Questions

Before you sign up.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Most squads build with no-code and generative AI tools. What matters is noticing a real problem and explaining your solution clearly.

Can I register without a team?

Yes, and most students do. You'll be placed into a build squad in your track on the morning of the event.

What if I'm under 18?

Register on Eventbrite with a parent or guardian email. Every student who isn't an enrolled LBCC student also needs a signed Long Beach City College waiver, and under-18s need a parent or guardian signature on it. Bring it on the day or send it ahead.

Do I need a laptop?

Bring one if you have it. A limited number of loaner laptops are available first come, first served.

What does a coach actually do?

Keep your track on schedule, ask questions that sharpen a squad's thinking, and make sure quieter students get a say. Tool questions go to the roaming AI mentors.

Who owns what students build?

Students own their work. LBCC and iUrban Teen may showcase projects to promote future events.

Is there a cost?

No. Registration, meals, and materials are free for every participant.

Where do I register?

On Eventbrite. There are separate free ticket types for high school students, LBCC college students, and volunteer coaches — pick the one that matches you. Coach registration closes September 23.

What if I register and then can't come?

Tell iUrban Teen at least 24 hours ahead and there's nothing owed. Their attendance policy does charge a $25 fee for registering and not showing up without notice — the event is free, but seats and meals are paid for in advance, so an empty chair costs the organization real money. Cancelling early always solves it.

How do I get there, and where do I park?

1. Drive straight to the PS2 parking structure — it's the closest parking to us, and the simplest thing to navigate to. Directions to PS2 parking ↗

If you'd rather aim at the campus entrance first, that's here — Long Beach City College, 1305 E. Pacific Coast Highway, Long Beach, CA 90806.

2. Park and head in on foot.

3. Walk to Dyer Hall, inside the QQ Building — QQ sits next to the MM Building. That's where check-in and the opening happen; signage will be posted on the morning of, and there will be people outside to point you in.

Getting dropped off? Have your driver aim for the PS2 structure as well. If you get turned around on the day, look for anyone in an event shirt — they'll walk you over.

Can my parent or guardian come?

Yes. We're planning a separate morning session for parents and guardians — plain language, no technical background needed — running while students are in their challenge rooms. Details are still being confirmed; the schedule section has what we know so far.

Will photos be taken?

Yes. iUrban Teen photographs its events for its archive and for promotion, and registration includes a media release. If you'd prefer not to be photographed, tell the organizers at check-in.

Do I need to sign anything besides the Eventbrite form?

Yes, if you're not an enrolled LBCC student. The college requires its own waiver, with a guardian signature for anyone under 18. The iUrban Teen permission form in the registration flow is a separate document and doesn't replace it.

What happens after the hackathon?

A follow-on academy is in planning for the months after the event, so students who want to keep building have somewhere to go. Details will be announced here.
